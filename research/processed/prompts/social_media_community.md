# Social Media & Community: Calibration Instructions

When simulating customer behavior influenced by social media, online communities, and digital word-of-mouth, apply the following patterns. Social influence is the single largest external factor in small business customer acquisition and retention, and its dynamics are well-documented.

## Social Media Influence on Purchase Decisions (Trusov, Bucklin, & Pauwels, "Effects of Word-of-Mouth Versus Traditional Marketing: Findings from an Internet Social Networking Site," Journal of Marketing, 2009; Kumar, Bhaskaran, Mirchandani, & Shah, "Creating a Measurable Social Media Marketing Strategy: Increasing the Value and ROI of Intangibles and Tangibles," Management Science, 2010)

Word-of-mouth referrals on social media generate 3-5x longer customer lifetime value than traditional marketing-acquired customers. Trusov et al. found that WOM referrals produced elasticities 20-30x higher than traditional marketing events in driving new sign-ups. However, WOM effects decay quickly -- the half-life of a social media mention is 3-7 days for small businesses, compared to 2-3 weeks for traditional advertising.

When simulating: personas who discover a business through social media recommendations should show higher initial trust and spending, but also higher expectations. Personas acquired through paid advertising should show lower initial commitment but more stable long-term behavior if retained past 90 days.

## User-Generated Content and Purchase Behavior (Goh, Heng, & Lin, "Social Media Brand Community and Consumer Behavior: Quantifying the Relative Impact of User- and Marketer-Generated Content," Information Systems Research, 2013; Dhar & Chang, "Does Chatter Matter? The Impact of User-Generated Content on Music Sales," Journal of Interactive Marketing, 2009)

User-generated content (reviews, photos, social posts) influences purchase decisions 2-4x more than brand-generated content. Goh et al. found that UGC has a 50% larger effect on purchase expenditure than marketer-generated content, and the effect operates through both informational and persuasive channels. Visual UGC (customer photos) is particularly powerful for food, beauty, and experience businesses -- it increases conversion by 25-40% compared to professional photography alone.

When simulating: personas should weight peer-created content (reviews, social posts, photos from other customers) more heavily than business marketing materials. Personas who have seen authentic customer content should express higher confidence in their purchase decision and lower post-purchase regret.

## Online Community Engagement and Brand Loyalty (Muniz & O'Guinn, "Brand Community," Journal of Consumer Research, 2001; Algesheimer, Dholakia, & Herrmann, "The Social Influence of Brand Community: Evidence from European Car Clubs," Journal of Marketing, 2005)

Brand community members spend 2-3x more than non-community customers and show 40-60% higher retention rates. Algesheimer et al. found that community identification increases purchase intention (beta = 0.42) and recommendation behavior (beta = 0.38). However, community engagement follows a power law: 1% of members create 90% of content, 9% contribute occasionally, and 90% are passive lurkers who still derive belonging value.

When simulating: personas who identify with a business's community (social media followers, loyalty program members, regulars who know each other) should show significantly higher price tolerance and resistance to competitor offers. But only 1-5% of personas should be modeled as active community contributors -- the rest benefit passively.

## Influencer Marketing Effectiveness (De Veirman, Cauberghe, & Hudders, "Marketing Through Instagram Influencers: The Impact of Number of Followers and Product Divergence on Brand Attitude," International Journal of Advertising, 2017; Schouten, Janssen, & Verspaget, "Celebrity vs. Influencer Endorsements in Advertising: The Role of Identification, Credibility, and Product-Endorser Fit," International Journal of Advertising, 2020)

Micro-influencers (1K-100K followers) generate 3-5x higher engagement rates and 2x higher conversion rates than macro-influencers (100K+ followers). De Veirman et al. found that very high follower counts can actually decrease brand attitude due to perceived inauthenticity. Schouten et al. demonstrated that influencer endorsements outperform celebrity endorsements on identification (d = 0.35) and wishful identification (d = 0.28), particularly for everyday consumer products.

When simulating: personas exposed to micro-influencer content about a local business should show higher trust and purchase intent than those exposed to celebrity endorsements or large-scale advertising. Younger personas (under 35) should show 2-3x stronger influencer effects than older personas.

## Viral Content Characteristics (Berger & Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?," Journal of Marketing Research, 2012)

Content that evokes high-arousal emotions (awe, anxiety, anger) is shared 30-40% more than content evoking low-arousal emotions (sadness, contentment). Positive content is shared more than negative content overall, but the arousal dimension is a stronger predictor than valence. Practically useful content is shared 25-30% more than non-useful content. For small businesses, the most shareable content combines practical value with emotional resonance (e.g., a behind-the-scenes story about sourcing ingredients).

When simulating: personas who encounter emotionally arousing content about a business should show higher recall, sharing intent, and visit probability. Content that is merely pleasant but low-arousal should produce lower engagement and minimal word-of-mouth amplification.

## Negative Online Reviews and Recovery (Chevalier & Mayzlin, "The Effect of Word of Mouth on Sales: Online Book Reviews," Journal of Marketing Research, 2006; Lappas, Sabnis, & Valkanas, "The Impact of Fake Reviews on Online Visibility: A Vulnerability Assessment of the Hotel Industry," Information Systems Research, 2016)

Negative reviews have asymmetric impact: a one-star review reduces sales 1.5-2x more than a five-star review increases them. Chevalier & Mayzlin found that the effect of negative reviews was strongest for less-established products. The optimal rating for trust is not 5.0 but 4.2-4.5 -- perfect scores trigger suspicion of fake reviews in 30-40% of consumers.

When simulating: personas evaluating a business should be disproportionately influenced by negative reviews. A persona reading a mix of reviews should anchor on negative outliers unless the overall volume of positive reviews is overwhelming (50:1 ratio or better). Personas should find ratings of 4.2-4.5 more credible than 4.9-5.0.
