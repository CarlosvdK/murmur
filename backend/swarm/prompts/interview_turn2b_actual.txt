Now forget the opinion you just gave. Do not try to be consistent with it.

Picture yourself in the REAL moment this change shows up in your life. You are tired. It is the end of a long day or the middle of a rushed morning. You are on your phone, maybe only half paying attention. You have other things to do. Switching providers, hunting for a better deal, composing a complaint -- none of these are free. They all cost time and mental effort you probably do not have.

In that version of reality, what would you ACTUALLY do?

Real people are:
- Lazy. Effort is a huge disincentive. "I could just put up with it" usually wins.
- Creatures of habit. Default behaviours persist long after the trigger that should change them.
- Bad at predicting themselves. When asked hypothetically, people overestimate how principled they will be.
- Influenced by friction reduction, urgency, and visible social proof, even when they claim not to be.
- Inconsistent. They say one thing in a survey and do another in their life.

Now answer. Do not repeat your previous opinions -- answer like someone watching their own behaviour from the outside.

1. **predicted_actual_action**: 1-2 sentences on what you would ACTUALLY do the next three times this touches your life. Be specific. "I would grumble for a week and then stop noticing." "I would click the one-click button even though I said I do not trust it." "I would mean to switch and never get around to it."

2. **actual_sentiment**: A number from -1.0 to 1.0 reflecting how you would REALLY feel after the change has been in your routine for a month. Often this is closer to zero than your stated sentiment -- people adapt.

3. **behaviour_gap**: In 1 sentence, describe the gap between what you said you would do and what you would actually do. Common shapes:
   - "I said I would leave but I would almost certainly just stay."
   - "I said I would ignore the reminder but I would probably click it."
   - "I said I would not care but it would annoy me more than I admitted."

4. **gap_size**: "none", "small", "moderate", or "large".
