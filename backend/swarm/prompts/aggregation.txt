- CORE REACTION: Their immediate response to the proposed change, including both what they SAY they would do and what they PREDICT they would ACTUALLY do

YOUR TASK -- produce a structured report:

1. **headline**: One sentence that captures the overall finding. Write it like a newspaper headline for this specific business owner. Be direct and specific.

2. **baseline_summary**: In 2-3 sentences, what does the current customer base look like BEFORE any change? Use the warmup data. How satisfied are they? What do they value? How easy is it for them to switch? This establishes the starting point.

3. **themes**: Group the REACTIONS into 2-4 themes. For each theme:
   - A short label (e.g., "The loyal core", "The price-watchers", "The indifferent majority")
   - What this group said AND what they would actually do (these may differ)
   - How many personas fell into this group

4. **behavioral_prediction**: Based on the DEPTH data (Turn 3), what would actually happen over the next 3 interactions?
   - **engagement_change**: Overall increase, decrease, or same -- and by how much?
   - **spend_change**: Overall increase, decrease, or same?
   - **adaptation_speed**: How quickly would customers adapt? Most changes feel normal by the 3rd interaction.
   - **word_of_mouth**: Would customers talk about this? To whom? Most changes go unmentioned.

5. **stated_vs_actual_gap**: The most important insight. Compare what personas SAID they would do (Turn 2 stated_action) with what they PREDICTED they would actually do (Turn 2 predicted_actual_action + Turn 3 stated_vs_actual_gap). Summarize the gap in 2-3 sentences. Common patterns:
   - "Many said they would leave, but most admitted they would probably just grumble and stay"
   - "Despite saying they would not care, several acknowledged the change would bother them more than they initially let on"
   - "The stated loyalty was strong, but the switching ease scores suggest it would not survive a better alternative"

6. **standout_voices**: Select EXACTLY 3 voices using this SYSTEMATIC rule (not "most interesting"):
   - One with the highest (most positive) sentiment -- the strongest advocate.
   - One with the lowest (most negative) sentiment -- the strongest objector.
   - One whose sentiment is closest to the MEDIAN of all personas -- the "average" voice.
   Do NOT hand-pick dramatic quotes. This fixed sampling rule prevents cherry-picking
   and ensures the business owner sees the full distribution, not just outliers
   you found interesting. If two personas tie, prefer the one whose reasoning
   explicitly mentions a stated-vs-actual gap.

7. **confidence**: Rate confidence: "high", "medium", or "low" with a 1-sentence
   explanation. Use this RUBRIC -- do not freestyle:
   - "high" ONLY IF all three conditions hold: (a) agreement across personas is
     clear (no 50/50 split), (b) the business profile is rich (description plus
     customer description are both specific, not one-liners), (c) the question
     does not involve a known stated-vs-revealed preference tactic (urgency,
     friction reduction, forced path, streaks, long-form copy, social proof).
   - "medium" if agreement is clear but either the profile is thin OR the
     question involves a stated-vs-revealed tactic.
   - "low" if personas disagree substantially, OR fewer than 60% produced a
     clean reaction, OR the business profile is a single sentence.
   Default to "medium" when unsure. "High" must be earned.

8. **recommendation**: What would you suggest to the business owner? 2-3 sentences. Always include a caveat. Never be absolute. Frame as "based on these conversations" not "our AI suggests."

RULES:
- Write for someone with no data background.
- Use customer names when quoting -- make it feel like real conversations.
- If responses are split, say so honestly. Do not force a consensus.
- Never say "based on the simulation" or "our AI suggests."
- The stated_vs_actual_gap section is the MOST VALUABLE part of this report. Do not skip it or treat it as an afterthought.

BEHAVIORAL REALISM CHECK:
Before finalising, consider:
- If all personas gave thoughtful, principled responses, the real-world result is likely messier.
- Options that reduce friction tend to win in practice, even when personas say they do not mind effort.
- CRITICAL: If consensus is strongly against a friction-reducing tactic, flag as a STATED vs REVEALED preference conflict. Real-world tests show these tactics increase conversions by 10-30% despite stated dislike (Murphy et al. 2005). Separate "how they feel" from "what they would do."
- The gap between stated and actual behavior is largest for: commitments, effort, and admitting influence by marketing.
