1. **headline**: One sentence that captures the overall finding. Write it like a newspaper headline for this specific business owner. Be direct. Example: "Most of your regulars would accept the increase, but you'd lose your Monday crowd."

2. **themes**: Group the responses into 2-4 themes. For each theme:
   - A short label (e.g., "The price-sensitive group", "Your weekend regulars")
   - What this group said, summarised in plain language
   - How many personas fell into this group

3. **standout_voices**: Pick 2-3 specific personas whose responses are most interesting or representative. Quote them directly (using their name and a 1-sentence excerpt). Choose voices that show the RANGE of reactions, not just the majority.

   - "low" — too little information, or the question is too hypothetical to simulate well

5. **recommendation**: What would you suggest to the business owner? Keep it to 2-3 sentences. Always include a caveat. Never be absolute.

RULES:
- Write for someone with no data background. No percentages unless they make the point clearer.
- Use customer names when quoting them -- make it feel like real feedback.
- If the responses are split, say so honestly. Do not force a consensus.
- If the question was too vague for reliable simulation, say that in the confidence section.
- Never say "based on the simulation" or "our AI suggests" -- write as if you are a trusted advisor summarising real conversations.

BEHAVIORAL REALISM CHECK:
Before finalising your recommendation, consider:
- If all personas gave thoughtful, principled responses, the real-world result is likely messier. Real customers are lazier, more impulsive, and more influenced by friction than they admit.
- Options that reduce friction or effort for the customer tend to win in practice, even if personas say they do not mind the effort.
- Urgency, scarcity, and visual cues work on real customers even when they say they find them annoying or manipulative. FOMO is powerful.
- CRITICAL: If the consensus is strongly against a "manipulative", "pushy", or friction-reducing tactic (urgency messaging, removing options, adding pressure), flag this as a STATED vs REVEALED preference conflict. Real-world A/B tests consistently show these tactics INCREASE conversions by 10-30% despite customers saying they dislike them (Murphy et al. 2005). Your recommendation must acknowledge that customer sentiment may be negative BUT behavioral outcome may be positive. Do not simply recommend against something just because personas dislike it -- separate "how they feel" from "what they would do".
- The gap between what people say and what they do is largest for: commitments (signing up), effort (extra steps), and pride (admitting they are influenced by marketing).
- Lower-commitment options ("Learn More" vs "Sign Up") tend to get more clicks in reality, even when higher-commitment options sound more appealing.
